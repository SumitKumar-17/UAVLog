SEMANTIC LOG ANALYTICS [UAV LOG]: VIDEO SCRIPT (about 7 minutes)
Sumit Kumar (22CS30056), Advanced Database Management Systems (CS60113) Term Project

This is the only script. 41 short steps, about 517 spoken words, roughly 7 minutes. The project PPT is submitted separately.

HOW TO RECORD
  1. Open the site (the hosted address, or the local one), wait for the Overview to load, and click Tour in the top bar. It starts on the quick path.
  2. Press Enter to do each step's action (it types or clicks for you) and move on; the arrow keys go back and forward.
  3. Press H to hide the card so only the spotlight shows (drag the card by its top edge if it covers something). N shows the narration under the card.
  4. Say the SAY line in your own words. <angle brackets> say what the action is. Press F to switch to the full 90-step tour if you want more.
  The hosted site is read-only: uploads and notes are off.

======================================================================

[1] A database problem
Show: UAV flight logs: 300 real PX4 flights, 70.7 million telemetry samples and over three thousand
      text events, all in one PostgreSQL.
SAY:  A drone writes a log on every flight. I treated the whole collection as a database problem:
      seventy million samples, three thousand events and two autopilots' vocabularies, in one
      PostgreSQL.

[2] Ctrl+K   <Click here>
Show: The command palette jumps to any page, and turns what you type into a search or a question.
SAY:  Control K opens a palette. It jumps to any page, and it also searches the events as you type.

[3] Type into the palette   <Type this>
Show: Type a few words, for example battery low.
SAY:  I type battery low.

[4] Result: matching events
Show: The palette ran the hybrid search while you typed. Each row is a real event with its flight
      and time; Enter opens the flight at that moment. The first two rows turn the words into a full
      search or a question.
SAY:  As I type, the matching events appear, each linking to the exact moment in a flight.

[5] Close the palette   <Press Esc>
Show: Press Escape to close it.
SAY:  Escape closes it.

======================================================================
ONE FLIGHT
======================================================================

[6] One flight
Show: Board, firmware, duration and the pilot's notes from the original log, then everything the
      database knows about this flight.
SAY:  This is one flight: a CZI board, twelve minutes fifty-four, and the parachute was used.

[7] Flight modes and events   <Hover the marks>
Show: The ribbon is the flight mode through time. Red triangles are critical events and yellow ticks
      are warnings. Drag across any chart to zoom this window.
SAY:  The ribbon is the mode through time and the marks are events; the outlined window shows what
      the charts are zoomed to.

[8] Behaviour strip
Show: Each 10 seconds of this flight compared with the same moments in every other flight: blue is
      typical, red is unusual. One vector search per window.
SAY:  Every ten seconds are compared with their five nearest windows in all other flights, one
      vector search each, and shown as a percentile. Red means unlike anything else.

[9] Click the most unusual moment   <Click here>
Show: The most unusual windows are listed under the strip. Clicking one jumps every chart to it and
      explains it.
SAY:  I click the most unusual moment and the charts jump to it.

[10] Why it is unusual
Show: The signal statistics furthest from the typical flight, in units of the typical spread: here
      the pitch and the position are changing in a way no normal flight does.
SAY:  The panel says why: these statistics are far from the corpus' typical value, for example a
      pitch of minus twenty-nine degrees.

[11] Replay the flight   <Click Replay>
Show: Press Replay: the time cursor sweeps every chart and the track, and events surface as it
      passes them. Space plays and pauses; the arrows step 5 seconds.
SAY:  Replay plays the flight back at sixteen times speed; every chart and the track follow the
      cursor.

[12] Pause the replay   <Click Pause>
Show: Pause where the failure begins.
SAY:  I pause at the failure.

[13] Incident report
Show: A report written from numbered evidence. The database computes the evidence with SQL; the
      model only writes prose and must cite it; citations that do not exist are removed.
SAY:  The numbers never come from the model: SQL gathers numbered evidence, the model writes prose
      and must cite it, and every citation is checked.

[14] Write the report   <Click here>
Show: One click gathers the evidence with SQL and asks the model to write from it. It takes a few
      seconds the first time and is saved afterwards.
SAY:  I ask for the incident report. The database gathers the evidence first.

[15] Click a citation   <Click here>
Show: A citation chip jumps the charts to the cited moment.
SAY:  Clicking E-six takes the charts to the failsafe moment.

[16] Similar moments
Show: Each flight is cut into overlapping 10-second windows, each embedded by how every signal
      behaved. Pick a moment, find where other flights behaved the same way.
SAY:  A second use of vectors: ten-second windows, embedded by signal behaviour, searched with an
      HNSW index.

[17] Find them   <Click here>
Show: Use the slider or press the button to search all flights.
SAY:  I search for moments like this one.

[18] Result: the matching moments
Show: One best window per other flight, closest first. Lower distance means more alike; the events
      inside each window are listed so you can see whether the match is meaningful.
SAY:  The nearest windows from other flights, with the events that happened in them.
